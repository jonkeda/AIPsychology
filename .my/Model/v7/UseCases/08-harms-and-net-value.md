# Harms and Net Value

Value is the benefit minus the damage. This document sets out the damage.

The question is not whether the model could conceivably be misused — anything can. It is whether the realistic pattern of use produces more harm than benefit, and whether the harmful uses are more attractive than the beneficial ones. On the second question the answer is uncomfortable.

## The structural problem

**The model's most harmful use is also its most attractive one.**

What people want from a framework about people is a way to categorise people. That is the appeal, and it is the thing this model cannot support. Every audience examined in this folder arrived at the same shape:

| Audience | What they will want | What is defensible |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Manager | Who on my team is behind? | What conditions am I creating? |
| HR | Assess our workforce | Do not assess your workforce, and here is what to measure instead |
| Individual | Which one am I? | Which domains am I talking about? |
| Coach | A framework to place clients in | Vocabulary for what clients describe |

In every case the wanted use is unsupported and the defensible use is less satisfying. A framework in that position does not fail gradually — it drifts toward its attractive use, because that is what its readers are looking for, and the disclaimers are read once.

**The model currently ships both.** It contains careful warnings against classification and a complete apparatus for classification, including an observation guide, per-position diagnostics, and a designed classifier. A reader who wants the prohibited use will find everything needed to perform it, in the same document that prohibits it.

This is not a documentation problem to be fixed with stronger warnings. It is a contents problem.

## Specific harms

### H1. The label that outlives its evidence

**Mechanism.** A practitioner records a position with moderate confidence and a note about the evidence. Within a fortnight the confidence and the evidence are gone and the label remains. The label is memorable, compact, and sounds analytical.

**Who is harmed.** The person labelled, in ways they will never see.

**Likelihood.** Very high. This is how all such labels behave, and the model's own guidance concedes that a position recorded without a confidence marker becomes a remembered fact.

**Severity.** Moderate to high, depending on who holds the label.

**Mitigation available.** Only one that works: do not produce labels about individuals. Confidence markers and re-assessment guidance are good practice and will not survive contact with ordinary working memory.

### H2. The unreliable distinction applied confidently

**Mechanism.** The model concedes that its two most consequential distinctions — open versus guarded evaluation, and identity-drawn versus competence-drawn boundaries — require repeated structured observation. Almost nobody will do repeated structured observation. The distinctions will therefore be made from single conversations, every time.

**Who is harmed.** Anyone told they are defending rather than judging, when they were judging correctly.

**Likelihood.** Near certain, if the distinctions are used at all.

**Severity.** Moderate. Higher when applied by someone with authority over the person.

**Mitigation.** Present the distinctions as questions to hold, never as determinations. This helps and does not solve it, because a named distinction invites a decision.

### H3. Legitimising a screening product

**Mechanism.** A published framework with structured constructs and an academic register is precisely what a vendor needs to build an employee AI-readiness assessment. The model's prohibitions do not travel with the constructs.

**Who is harmed.** Employees assessed by a derivative product, and possibly denied opportunity by it.

**Likelihood.** Moderate, and rising with the model's reach. This risk is created by the distribution strategy in [Plans/01-way-forward.md](../Plans/01-way-forward.md), not despite it.

**Severity.** High. Employment consequences at scale, from an unvalidated framework, with the model's name lending credibility.

**Mitigation.** Partial at best. Licence terms will not prevent it. What helps: not publishing a classification procedure, publishing the prohibitions prominently enough to be citable against a derivative, and declining to build an organisational assessment product.

**This is the most serious identified harm** and it is the one the project has least control over.

### H4. False confidence about a person's inner state

**Mechanism.** The descriptions are specific and vivid. Reading them produces a strong sense of recognition. Recognition feels like knowledge.

**Who is harmed.** The person misread, and the reader who stops enquiring because they believe they already know.

**Likelihood.** High. This is the expressiveness trap, and it is not avoidable by warning about it — the warning is read with the same brain that then feels the recognition.

**Severity.** Moderate.

**Mitigation.** The only real one is empirical: run the discriminability study. If the positions cannot be told apart, remove them. Until then the risk is unquantified.

### H5. Suppressing a correct objection

**Mechanism.** An engineer raises well-founded concerns about a tool. The concerns are read through the framework as guarded evaluation — a psychological posture rather than a technical finding — and the substance is not engaged with.

**Who is harmed.** The engineer, and the organisation that discarded accurate information.

**Likelihood.** Moderate. The model works hard against this, renaming the position specifically to avoid it, and the risk remains because the underlying temptation is strong.

**Severity.** High. Losing an organisation's most careful sceptic is expensive, and the loss is invisible.

**Mitigation.** The renaming helps. What would help more: stating explicitly and prominently that guarded evaluation is frequently correct, and that the first response to it should be to engage with the findings.

### H6. Delaying appropriate help

**Mechanism.** Genuine distress is read as a described psychological position with a typical course, and treated as a phase to be worked through rather than as something needing clinical attention.

**Who is harmed.** The person.

**Likelihood.** Low, but non-zero, particularly with career coaches lacking clinical training.

**Severity.** High where it occurs.

**Mitigation.** Referral guidance, made prominent rather than parenthetical. This is a genuinely effective mitigation and is largely already present.

### H7. Wasted effort

**Mechanism.** An organisation runs an assessment exercise that produces nothing actionable, and concludes the underlying issues are not worth attending to.

**Who is harmed.** The organisation, and by extension the people whose real problem went unaddressed.

**Likelihood.** Moderate.

**Severity.** Low to moderate. The second-order effect — discrediting the whole area — is the more damaging part.

## Harms the model does not have

Worth stating, because a harm audit that finds only harms is not an audit.

- **It is not clinical.** It does not diagnose disorders or recommend treatment.
- **It does not collect data by default.** The privacy-preserving architecture in the plans is genuine, not decorative.
- **It does not claim validation.** The epistemic honesty throughout is unusual and materially reduces the risk of it being trusted more than it deserves.
- **It does not recommend a target.** The descriptive stance, once resolved, removes the harm of pushing people toward a position.
- **Its prohibitions are real.** Several documented harms are addressed by explicit, specific prohibitions. This is more than most practitioner frameworks do.

## Net assessment

| Component | Benefit | Harm | Net |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Domain-specificity | High | Negligible | **Clearly positive** |
| Verification burden | High | Negligible | **Clearly positive** |
| Delegation levels | High | Negligible | **Clearly positive** |
| Identity stakes | Moderate–High | Low | **Positive** |
| The prohibition set | High | None | **Clearly positive** |
| Coerced calibration | Moderate–High | Low | **Positive** |
| Named experiences as vocabulary | Moderate | Low–Moderate | **Positive, with care** |
| The confusable distinctions | Low | Moderate | **Negative as tools, positive as questions** |
| Position graph | Low | Moderate | **Negative** |
| Classification apparatus | Very low | High | **Clearly negative** |
| Observation guide | Low | Moderate–High | **Negative in manager and HR hands** |

**The net is positive, and it is positive because of a specific subset.** The four high-benefit, low-harm components carry the model. Everything with meaningful harm potential also has low demonstrated benefit — which is a fortunate alignment, because it means the harm can be removed without losing much.

## The conclusion this forces

**The model is net-positive if it is cut down, and net-negative or marginal if it is shipped whole to the wrong audiences.**

The components that cause harm are the same components that contributed nothing to any scenario in [04-individuals.md](04-individuals.md) through [07-coaches-and-clinicians.md](07-coaches-and-clinicians.md). Removing them costs almost no value and removes most of the risk.

Three specific consequences:

1. **Do not publish a classification procedure.** Not the observation guide as a diagnostic instrument, not the classifier design, not per-position identification criteria. Publish descriptions of experiences; do not publish a method for assigning them to people.
2. **Package by audience.** The manager-facing and HR-facing material should contain the conditions, the prohibitions, and the vocabulary, and should not contain the diagnostic apparatus. Shipping everything to everyone hands the dangerous half to the readers least able to hold it safely.
3. **Make the prohibitions citable.** They need to be prominent enough that someone objecting to a derivative screening product can point at them. This is the only defence available against H3.

None of these reduces the model's benefit in any documented scenario. All of them substantially reduce its harm. That is an unusually clean trade and it should be taken.
