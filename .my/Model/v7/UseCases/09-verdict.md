# Verdict

The question was whether the model has enough value to justify continuing, and whether continuing would be wasteful.

## The answer

**The model has real value. It is narrower than its current size implies, and continuing to build it in its current direction would be wasteful.**

Three findings support this.

**Finding 1 — Four components carry almost all the value.** Domain-specificity, identity stakes, delegation levels, and verification burden appear in nearly every scenario where the model changes a decision. The fourteen-position graph, the attributes, and the classification apparatus appear in none. The effort and the value have come apart, and the parts that took the most work are the parts that contribute least.

**Finding 2 — Most components have strong prior art.** Oversight load restates the ironies of automation. Delegation levels restate levels-of-automation taxonomies. Calibrated use restates calibrated trust. Burnout has a mature literature the model should defer to entirely. This is initially deflating and ultimately the most useful finding in the audit, because it offers an evidence base at zero cost.

**Finding 3 — The harmful components are the low-value ones.** Everything with meaningful harm potential also contributed nothing to any documented scenario. Cutting the risk costs almost no benefit. That alignment is fortunate and should be acted on.

## The steelman

Before recommending cuts, the strongest case for the model as it stands.

**The synthesis is genuine work.** The relevant material is spread across human factors, occupational psychology, organisational change, and trust research. Nobody has assembled it for knowledge workers facing generative AI. Practitioner-facing synthesis of a scattered literature is a real contribution, even where every component is borrowed.

**Two claims may be genuinely new.** That positions are per-domain rather than per-person, and that identity stakes determine whether a person experiences adoption or distress. Automation and trust research generally treat the person-system relationship as singular. The extension to simultaneous, sharply different positions across one person's own task domains is not standard, and it is the model's best candidate for an original contribution.

**Generative replication is not execution automation.** The prior literature studied machines taking over execution while judgement stayed human. Generative systems replicate judgement and craft, which is why identity is the operative variable rather than workload. This is a real difference and it may mean the transfer of the older findings is non-trivial.

**The epistemic honesty is unusual and valuable in itself.** Most practitioner frameworks in circulation are equally unvalidated and say nothing about it. One that states its status, marks its conjectures, and specifies its own falsifiers is a better object than most of what it competes with, and that has value independent of whether the content is right.

**The prohibition set is under-recognised.** Organisations are currently doing several expensive and counterproductive things — tracking individual usage, mandating delegation levels, assessing workforce readiness with untested instruments. A reasoned argument against each, including a self-interested one, is worth more than most positive interventions available in this area.

**The graph was necessary even though it should now be demoted.** The four surviving ideas could not have been written first. They were found by building the whole thing and seeing what carried weight. Work that produces a distillate is not wasted work, even when most of it is discarded.

## The options

### Option A — Continue as planned

Complete V7, build the agents, build the website, publish the full model.

**Against.** Extends the components the audit found valueless, builds the classification apparatus the harm analysis identifies as the main risk, and ships everything to everyone. The website in particular is a large investment predicated on material that has not been read by anyone outside the project.

**Not recommended.**

### Option B — Stop

Archive the work.

**Against.** Discards genuine value. Four components are useful, low-risk, and not otherwise packaged for the people who need them. The prohibition set alone would be worth publishing. Stopping now means the distillation work — the expensive part — has been done and never delivered.

**Not recommended.**

### Option C — Ground, cut, and ship small

Reconcile with prior art, cut to what earns its place, publish something short and practical, and defer the tooling until someone outside the project has used the short version.

**Recommended.**

## The recommendation

### Step 1 — Reconcile with prior art

**Before anything else.** The highest-return action available: it costs reading time, requires nothing from anyone, and materially changes what the model is.

Concretely: replace `BO` Burnout and `CU` Calibrated Use with the established constructs. Reframe verification burden as the ironies of automation arriving in knowledge work. Cite the levels-of-automation lineage for delegation. Ground the Disruption family in identity threat research.

**What this achieves.** The model stops being *an untested framework of new constructs* and becomes *established constructs applied to a new context, with the application identified as the untested part*. That is a fundamentally stronger position and it is available for free.

### Step 2 — Run the discriminability check

The C1 study from [Plans/03-cheap-evidence.md](../Plans/03-cheap-evidence.md). An afternoon. It answers the one question the audit could not: whether the model discriminates or merely describes. A failure here would change the recommendation substantially, which is precisely why it should be run before the writing.

### Step 3 — Cut

Per [02-component-audit.md](02-component-audit.md): keep the four core components, the small vocabulary, the prohibitions, and coerced calibration. Demote the graph to background. Cut classification, integration styles, cultural variability, the advocacy overlay, and the cohort property. Fold oversight load into the verification burden axis. Demote the overuse drivers to a discussion checklist.

**Expected outcome: something close to a tenth of the current volume.** That is the point, not a side effect.

### Step 4 — Write the short version

One practitioner document. Four ideas, a small vocabulary, a prohibition set, and honest sourcing. Readable in one sitting and retainable afterwards.

**The full model set remains as the reference layer behind it,** frozen and citable. Nothing is deleted; the delivery changes.

### Step 5 — Package by audience

Three variants, differing in what they contain rather than in tone:

| Audience | Contains | Omits |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Individuals and coaches | Domain framing, the named experiences, delegation levels, referral boundary | Everything organisational |
| Managers | Conditions, sequencing, verification burden, prohibitions | Position descriptions, observation guide, anything diagnostic |
| HR and organisations | Prohibitions, measurement guidance, programme design | The diagnostic apparatus entirely |

### Step 6 — Give it to five people and wait

Coaches are the best first readers — they will apply vocabulary immediately and notice quickly when it does not fit.

**Then wait, and watch for one thing: does anyone use it a second time, unprompted?**

### Step 7 — Only then consider tooling

The agents and the website are deferred until Step 6 returns something. Both are substantial investments predicated on material nobody outside the project has used. If the short version does not get used twice, neither will the tool.

## The stopping rule

Stated in advance so the decision is not made on sentiment.

**Continue if,** after the short version has been in the hands of five to ten practitioners:

- At least two return to it unprompted, or
- At least one concrete decision changed because of it — a rollout sequenced differently, a metric not adopted, a review process redesigned — or
- Someone reports a case it clearly did not describe, and this is noticed

**Stop, or reduce to an archived write-up, if:**

- Everyone finds it interesting and nobody uses it twice
- Every case fits comfortably, which would confirm the expressiveness trap
- The discriminability check fails and cannot be fixed by collapsing positions
- It is used to justify decisions already made

**The first stop condition is the likely one and the easiest to miss.** Positive verbal feedback about a framework is close to worthless as evidence — people are polite about frameworks. Second unprompted use is the only signal that counts.

## On whether the work so far was wasted

It was not, and the distinction matters for deciding what to do next.

The audit's conclusions were only reachable by building the thing. The four components that carry the value could not have been identified without writing the ten that do not. The prior-art overlap could not have been seen without constructs specific enough to compare. The harm analysis required an apparatus concrete enough to be dangerous.

**What would be wasteful is continuing to elaborate the parts that did not survive.** Building the classifier, completing the graph, adding the collective layer, extending to new cohorts — all of that is investment in components the audit found valueless or harmful.

The work has produced something worth publishing. It is smaller than expected, better supported than expected once grounded, and more useful in a shorter form. The correct next action is to finish it by making it smaller, which is a less satisfying kind of progress than continuing to build, and is the right one.

## One-paragraph summary

> The framework contains four genuinely useful ideas — that a person's relationship with AI is per-domain rather than singular, that identity stakes determine whether adoption is uneventful or distressing, that delegation level is both a psychological and a cost variable, and that verification burden is the current under-named problem — together with a set of prohibitions that would save organisations from several expensive mistakes. Most of its other components either duplicate better-supported work or contribute nothing to any concrete use. It should be grounded in the automation and identity-threat literatures, cut to roughly a tenth of its size, published as a short practitioner document with the full model as a frozen reference, and given to a handful of coaches. If none of them return to it, the work should be archived and the answer accepted.
