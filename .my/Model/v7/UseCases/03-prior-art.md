# Prior Art

Whether the model describes something already described, and what that means for its value.

This document matters more than its position in the folder suggests. If the model's constructs already exist with evidence behind them, two things follow, and they pull in opposite directions: the model is less original than it appears, and it is far better supported than it appears. The second effect is larger.

**All references below are from recall and must be verified before publication.** Some may be misattributed or misdated. They are offered as leads for the reconciliation work, not as a citation list.

## The central finding

**The model has largely rediscovered the human factors and automation literature, in a new domain.**

That literature spent four decades on aviation, process control, and vehicle automation, and worked out in some detail what happens when a machine takes over part of a skilled person's task. The model arrives at strikingly similar conclusions from a different starting point.

This is not an accusation of derivativeness. Arriving independently at conclusions that a mature field reached is evidence that the observations are sound. But it does change what the model should claim, and it offers something the project badly needs and cannot otherwise obtain: **an evidence base, at no cost.**

## Component by component

### Verification burden and oversight load

**Prior art: strong and direct.**

Bainbridge's *Ironies of Automation* (1983) is the closest antecedent, and the overlap is close to complete. Its argument: automating the easy parts of a task leaves the human with monitoring, humans are poor at sustained monitoring, the skills required to intervene decay through disuse, and the operator is asked to take over precisely when the situation has become hard enough to defeat the automation.

Every element of `OL` Oversight Load is in that paper. The model's contribution is the observation that this now applies to knowledge work rather than to power stations.

Related and equally relevant: automation complacency and automation bias, developed extensively by Parasuraman, Manzey and others, describing exactly the rubber-stamping response — reduced scrutiny of automated output, and errors of omission where the person fails to catch what the machine got wrong.

**Implication.** This should be reframed as *the ironies of automation arriving in knowledge work*. The model's newest and least supported construct becomes its best supported one. This is the single highest-value change available to the project.

### Delegation levels

**Prior art: strong and direct.**

Levels-of-automation taxonomies date to Sheridan and Verplank in the late 1970s, and were substantially developed by Parasuraman, Sheridan and Wickens around 2000 into a framework distinguishing stages of automation — information acquisition, information analysis, decision selection, action implementation — each with its own degree of automation.

The model's D1–D5 is a coarser scale applied to generative AI. It is a reasonable application, not a new idea.

**Implication.** Cite the lineage and stop presenting the scale as novel. The genuinely current observation — that delegation level maps onto token cost, making the psychological discipline and the economic one the same discipline — is not in the prior literature, because the prior literature had no per-request marginal cost. That is worth keeping as the distinctive part.

### Calibrated use

**Prior art: strong.**

Lee and See's work on trust in automation (2004) developed *appropriate trust* or *calibrated trust* — trust matched to the system's actual capability — along with the failure modes of overtrust and distrust. This is `CU` Calibrated Use, with a better developed theory and empirical support behind it.

**Implication.** `CU` Calibrated Use should defer to calibrated trust rather than compete with it. The model's contribution is per-domain calibration, which is a genuine extension: trust research has generally treated trust as a property of a person-system pair rather than as varying across the same person's task domains.

### Identity shock and scope boundary

**Prior art: moderate to strong.**

Occupational identity threat is a developed area. Petriglieri's work on identity threat responses (around 2011) distinguishes strategies for protecting a threatened identity from strategies for restructuring it — a distinction that maps closely onto `SB` Scope Boundary versus `IP` Integrated Practice.

Adjacent and relevant: self-efficacy under capability challenge, and the deskilling literature in the labour process tradition, which examined skill devaluation through automation from a different angle.

**Implication.** The Disruption family should be grounded in identity threat research. The model's contribution is the domain-specific framing and the application to capability replication rather than to role loss.

### Guarded evaluation

**Prior art: strong, from two directions.**

Identity-protective cognition and motivated reasoning describe the asymmetric updating pattern precisely, with substantial experimental support. Separately, technology resistance research within the acceptance tradition has examined resistance as a rational response to threat rather than as a deficit.

**Implication.** The construct is well supported. The model's version adds the domain-specificity and the reframing away from "resistance", which is a presentational contribution rather than a theoretical one.

### Burnout

**Prior art: extensive, and the model should be careful here.**

Burnout is a mature construct with validated instruments and a large literature, most usefully the job demands-resources tradition, which frames it in terms of demands exceeding resources — a framing that accommodates verification burden without needing anything new.

**Implication.** `BO` Burnout should defer entirely. Redefining an established clinical-adjacent construct inside a small unvalidated framework is a liability, and an occupational health specialist would object to it immediately. The model should use the established construct and describe how its conditions produce it.

### Skill atrophy

**Prior art: moderate, and growing.**

Cognitive offloading research, and specifically work on how externalising memory and reasoning affects retention, is directly relevant. The automation literature's treatment of skill decay under monitoring roles is older and more developed.

**Implication.** The model's position — that this is the largest open question and it will not take a view — is defensible but slightly overstated. The question is less open than the model assumes; there is relevant work, and it should be consulted before the gap is declared.

### Adoption trajectory

**Prior art: extensive.**

Technology acceptance research, diffusion of innovations, and the transtheoretical model are all acknowledged in the model's own lineage document. The model's stated departure — that intensity is modulated by identity stakes — is a real one.

## What survives as distinctive

Stripping out everything with strong prior art leaves a smaller but genuine contribution:

| Surviving claim | Why it is not already covered |
| --- | --- |
| **Per-domain position** | Automation and trust research generally treat the person-system relationship as singular. The claim that the same person holds sharply different positions across their own task domains, simultaneously, is not standard |
| **Identity stakes as the intensity modulator** | Acceptance research explains adoption; identity threat research explains distress. Linking them so that stakes predict which of the two applies is a connection worth making |
| **Application to generative capability replication** | The automation literature studied machines taking over *execution*. Generative systems replicate *judgement and craft*, which is what makes identity the operative variable rather than workload |
| **Delegation level as a joint psychological and economic axis** | Genuinely new, because prior automation contexts had no per-request marginal cost |
| **The synthesis and packaging** | The relevant material is spread across human factors, occupational psychology, and organisational change. Nobody has assembled it for knowledge workers facing generative AI |

That last row is the most honest description of the project's value, and it is a legitimate contribution. Practitioner-facing synthesis of a scattered literature is real work with real utility. It is a much more modest claim than the model currently makes, and it is defensible in a way the current framing is not.

## What this does to the value calculation

**The bad news.** The model is substantially less original than it presents itself as being. Several of its components would be improved by deletion and replacement with a citation. An expert reviewer from human factors would identify the overlap within minutes, and the model's current framing does not acknowledge it.

**The good news, which is larger.** The project's central problem is that it has no evidence and cannot afford any. Reconciliation with prior art solves a substantial part of that problem for free. A framework built from established constructs, applied to a new domain, with the *application* identified as the untested part, is in a fundamentally different position from a framework of invented constructs with no support at all.

The difference in what can honestly be claimed:

| Before reconciliation | After reconciliation |
| --- | --- |
| An untested framework of new constructs | Established constructs applied to a new context |
| Nothing can be relied on | The components are supported; the application and the domain-specificity claim are the untested parts |
| Validation requires testing everything | Validation narrows to what is genuinely new |
| A reviewer's first question is "why should anyone believe this?" | A reviewer's first question is "does the transfer hold?" — a much better question to be asked |

**This is the highest-return work available to the project.** It costs reading time, it requires no participants, no money, and no permission, and it materially changes what the model is.

## Practical consequences

1. **Do the reconciliation before publishing anything.** Publishing first and being corrected by a reviewer is a recoverable but costly error.
2. **Replace rather than rename.** Where an established construct covers the ground better, use it and cite it. `BO` Burnout and `CU` Calibrated Use are the clearest candidates.
3. **Reframe verification burden as the headline application of automation ironies to knowledge work.** This is the strongest available positioning.
4. **Narrow the claimed contribution** to per-domain positions, identity stakes as modulator, generative replication as distinct from execution automation, and the synthesis.
5. **Expect the model to shrink further.** That is the intended outcome.

## The risk of not doing this

An unaffiliated author publishing a framework that reinvents a well-known literature without citing it will be dismissed, and the dismissal will be fair. The model's genuine contributions would go with it. Given that the reconciliation costs nothing but reading, not doing it would be the single most avoidable failure available to the project.
