# Using the Model Without Validation

The model has no empirical support. This document sets out how it can still be used honestly, which uses are safe, which are not, and what test separates them.

The short version: an unvalidated model is a **structured way of paying attention**. It is not a measurement. Every legitimate use follows from that distinction, and every illegitimate use ignores it.

## The test that decides every case

Before any use of the model, one question:

> **If the model is wrong, what happens to the person it was applied to?**

| Answer | Verdict |
| --- | --- |
| A conversation went in a slightly unhelpful direction | Fine. Proceed |
| Someone was offered support they did not need | Fine, if the support was optional and non-stigmatising |
| Someone was left alone when they wanted help | Acceptable risk, if the model was not the only route to help |
| Someone was labelled in a way that followed them | Not permitted |
| Someone's pay, role, promotion, or job changed | Not permitted, under any circumstances |

The test is about **reversibility and consequence**, not about confidence. A practitioner who feels certain about a classification is in exactly the same epistemic position as one who does not, because neither has an instrument. Confidence is not evidence, and the model provides no mechanism by which it could become evidence.

This test is more useful than a list of permitted uses, because it generalises to situations no list anticipated.

## Three tiers of claim

Everything the model can be asked to do falls into one of three tiers. The tier determines what is required to use it.

### Tier A — Vocabulary and framing

**Safe. Use freely.**

- Naming a pattern so two people can discuss it: *"that sounds like guarded evaluation rather than a considered judgement about the tool."*
- The per-domain framing: insisting that "how does she feel about AI" be replaced with "in which domain, and what is at stake there."
- The delegation vocabulary: D1 through D5 as a way of being precise about what was handed over.
- The questions themselves, used as prompts for reflection rather than as items in an instrument.

**Why this is safe.** A vocabulary is falsified by being useless, not by being wrong. If "identity shock" does not describe anything real, people will stop using the word. No one is harmed in the meantime. This tier is where nearly all of the model's current value sits, and it is available now.

**The strongest single claim in this tier** is the domain framing, and it is worth stating separately because it survives even if every position in the model is wrong: *a person does not have one relationship with AI, they have several, and treating them as one produces bad conversations.* That observation is nearly free of theoretical commitment and it changes how people talk.

### Tier B — Pattern recognition and conditions

**Use with discipline. Record uncertainty. Never attach consequences.**

- Recognising that several people on a team appear to be in a similar position, and asking what in the environment might explain it.
- Noticing that a stated reason for disengagement may not be the operative one, and running the reversibility check.
- Identifying that verification burden has risen and asking whether the workflow is the problem rather than the people.
- Using the position descriptions to generate hypotheses about what a person might find difficult.

**What discipline means here, concretely:**

1. **Record confidence with every classification.** A position noted without a confidence marker becomes a remembered fact within a fortnight. The marker is not decoration; it is the only thing preventing a guess from hardening.
2. **Record the evidence, not just the conclusion.** *"`GE` Guarded Evaluation — moderate confidence — discounted two positive examples unprompted, no counter-example available when asked"* is a usable note. *"`GE` Guarded Evaluation"* is not.
3. **Hold classifications loosely and revisit them.** Anything that changes the environment invalidates them.
4. **Never share the classification with the person being classified during an assessment.** Once someone knows the vocabulary, subsequent observation measures how well they read the model.

### Tier C — Measurement, prediction, and consequence

**Not available. Not with caveats, not with disclaimers, not with a signed acknowledgement.**

- Placing a person in a position as a matter of record.
- Predicting what someone will do.
- Scoring, ranking, or comparing people.
- Any input to performance management, promotion, hiring, redundancy selection, or capability process.
- Clinical assessment or anything presented as such.
- Screening — deciding who gets an opportunity based on an assessed position.

**Why disclaimers do not rescue this tier.** Once a classification enters a system that has consequences, the disclaimer is read once and the label is read forever. The practical question is not whether the model claims to measure but whether the output is *used* as a measurement, and a position code in a spreadsheet next to a person's name will be used as a measurement whatever the accompanying text says.

The operational rule that implements this: **if a classification could plausibly end up in a system that affects someone's employment, do not create the classification.**

## What "unvalidated" actually means here

Precision matters, because the word covers several different weaknesses and they have different implications.

| Weakness | What is unknown | What it blocks |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **No reliability data** | Whether two people would classify the same case the same way | Any use where consistency matters — comparison across people, across time, across assessors |
| **No construct validity** | Whether the positions correspond to anything that exists | Any claim that a position is a real property of a person rather than a description of behaviour |
| **No predictive validity** | Whether position predicts anything later | Every forward-looking use, including all intervention targeting |
| **No norms** | What is typical | Any statement of the form "unusually high" or "more than most people" |
| **No cross-cultural data** | Whether any of it transfers | Application outside the contexts it was written from |

The model is weak in all five. The important consequence is that it cannot support **comparison** — between people, between teams, or between the same person at two points in time. Nearly every misuse is a comparison in disguise.

## What survives the weakness

It would be easy to conclude that nothing is usable. That is over-correction, and it wastes something genuinely valuable.

Four things survive:

**The questions are good independently of the model.** *"If a better model came out next month that was good at the part you keep for yourself, what would you do?"* is a useful question to ask a professional. It is useful whether or not `SB` Scope Boundary and `IP` Integrated Practice: mastery are distinct constructs. The interview protocol has value that does not depend on the classification scheme it was built to serve.

**The structural observations are close to self-evident.** That mandated adoption produces compliance rather than judgement; that forcing delegation beyond what someone accepts produces resistance later rather than never; that a person can be fluent in one area and defensive in another. These are not strong theoretical claims and they do not need validation to be worth acting on.

**The conditions are real even if the positions are not.** Identity stakes, delegation level, and verification burden are describable features of a work situation. Verification burden in particular is directly measurable from workflow data. A practitioner who ignores the position graph entirely and uses only the axes still has something useful.

**Being wrong in public is productive.** A specific, falsifiable, freely available model attracts correction. A vague one does not. The model's specificity is an asset precisely because it makes the model easy to attack.

## Language that keeps the use honest

The difference between acceptable and unacceptable use is often visible in a single sentence. These substitutions are not cosmetic.

| Avoid | Use |
| --- | --- |
| "She is in `GE` Guarded Evaluation" | "Her responses about this domain look like guarded evaluation" |
| "He needs to move to `CU` Calibrated Use" | "The conditions that would make his current position uncomfortable are X and Y" |
| "The team is mostly `OU` Overuse" | "Several people describe usage patterns that fit the overuse description" |
| "The assessment shows..." | "The conversation suggested..." |
| "This is a validated framework" | "This is a working framework with no empirical support yet" |
| "Your position is..." | Nothing. Do not report positions to the person assessed |

## Disclosure

Anyone on the receiving end of the model is entitled to know its status. A single paragraph, used consistently:

> This framework is a set of descriptions of how people respond to AI in their work. It has not been tested and may be wrong. It is being used here to structure a conversation, not to assess or measure anyone. Nothing from this conversation goes into any record that affects work, pay, or role. Participation is optional and can stop at any point.

If that paragraph cannot honestly be said in a given context, the model should not be used in that context. This is a useful practical filter — it catches most of the bad cases without requiring anyone to reason about them.

## Where the model will mislead, even in Tier A

Recorded so the failure is anticipated rather than discovered.

- **On a single conversation.** `OE` Open Evaluation versus `GE` Guarded Evaluation, and `SB` Scope Boundary versus `IP` Integrated Practice: mastery, require repeated observation by construction. A single sitting cannot separate them, and a practitioner who believes it can has misunderstood the model rather than applied it.
- **Under a mandate.** Where AI use is measured and enforced, usage tells nothing about adoption and enthusiasm tells nothing about enthusiasm.
- **On anyone who has read the model.** The answers become a performance of the framework. This will worsen as the model circulates, which is a real cost of the distribution strategy in [01-way-forward.md](01-way-forward.md) and should be accepted deliberately rather than discovered later.
- **Outside scope.** Assistive users, people who are subjects of AI rather than users of it, and AI-native practitioners. The vocabulary will produce confident misdiagnosis, because it is expressive enough to describe anyone.

That last point deserves emphasis. The model's descriptions are rich enough that almost any person can be made to fit one of them. **Fitting is not evidence.** A framework that explains every case explains none, and the discipline of noticing cases that do not fit is the main thing standing between this model and astrology.
