# Articles

What to write for each audience, what each piece must not claim, and where it goes. Articles are the main distribution mechanism in [01-way-forward.md](01-way-forward.md) — the model is unlikely to be found any other way.

## Principles for all of them

**One idea per article.** The model is large. Articles that attempt to convey all of it convey none of it. Each piece below carries a single idea and mentions the rest only as a link.

**The epistemic status appears early and is not apologetic.** A sentence near the top: *this is a framework, not a finding; it has not been tested.* Readers who care will respect it, and readers who do not will not have been misled.

**No position labels applied to real named people.** Not in examples, not in anecdotes, not about public figures.

**Composite examples, declared as composites.** Illustrative cases must be constructed and said to be constructed. Presenting a composite as a real case is a small deception that would undermine the honesty the whole project depends on.

**Land on a question, not an instruction.** The model is descriptive. Articles that end with "here is what to do about your team" have abandoned that in the last paragraph, which is where readers pay most attention.

# The article set

## A1. The domain framing — for a general professional audience

**The single idea.** A person does not have one relationship with AI. They have several, one per area of their work, and treating them as one produces bad conversations.

**Why this is the first article.** It is the model's most defensible claim, it is close to self-evident once stated, it requires no position vocabulary at all, and it survives even if every other part of the model is wrong. It is also the observation most likely to change how a reader talks the same afternoon.

**Shape.** Open with the recognisable failure: a colleague described as "against AI" who is in fact fluent with it in three areas and firm about one. Explain why that one is different — the stakes are not the same. Introduce domains and identity stakes. Stop.

**Must not claim.** Any of the positions. This piece introduces no codes.

**Channel.** A general professional publication or a personal blog with wide syndication.

## A2. Guarded evaluation is not resistance — for engineering and product leaders

**The single idea.** The person carefully documenting the tool's failures is doing something different from the person refusing to try it, and the difference determines whether anything will help.

**Why this audience.** Engineering leaders are the group most often told to drive adoption and least well equipped with language for what they are encountering. They are also close to the phenomenon and will spot an inaccurate description immediately, which makes them a good early critical audience.

**Shape.** The asymmetric updating signature — failures remembered, successes attributed to prompt skill or an easy task. Why arguing with evidence does not work on it. Why it is not obstruction. What actually moves it, which is usually an event rather than an argument.

**Must not claim.** That guarded evaluation is a problem to be solved. The careful sceptic is frequently right, and an article that treats them as an obstacle would be both wrong and self-defeating.

## A3. The review trap — for engineering leaders and platform teams

**The single idea.** When agents produce more than a person can check, the bottleneck moves from production to verification, and the failure that follows is not an adoption failure.

**Why this may be the highest-impact article available.** It addresses a problem people are currently living through without a name for it, it is immediately recognisable, and the underlying construct is the most measurable thing in the model — review-to-production ratio comes out of ordinary workflow data.

**Shape.** The four responses when review volume exceeds capacity: rubber-stamping, refusing, quietly lowering the standard, or redesigning the workflow. Why only the last resolves anything. Why rubber-stamping is corrosive specifically because the person knows they are doing it. The measurement that anticipates it.

**Must not claim.** That the burden causes burnout. It is a stated conjecture with a specified test and it should be presented as one — which is also the article's best hook for a reader who might have the data to check it.

## A4. Delegation levels and what they cost — for software engineers

**The single idea.** D1 through D5 is simultaneously a psychological scale and a cost scale, and most work is delegated at a higher level than it needs to be.

**Why this audience responds.** It is concrete, it connects to something engineers already track, and it offers an immediately usable distinction. It also introduces the model's machinery through its least contentious component.

**Shape.** The five levels with real examples. The point at which authorship becomes genuinely ambiguous. The observation that delegation level correlates with token cost, so calibrating it is both a psychological and an economic discipline. A short link to [09-adoption-programme.md](09-adoption-programme.md).

**Must not claim.** That any level is correct. The claim is that the level should be chosen rather than defaulted to.

## A5. Why rollouts succeed and then unravel — for HR and organisational development

**The single idea.** Forcing delegation above what someone accepts produces compliance that looks like adoption, and it resurfaces later as disengagement or exit.

**Why this audience.** It explains a pattern they have seen and been unable to account for, and it arrives with an organisational remedy rather than an individual one.

**Shape.** The delegation ceiling. Coerced calibration and why it is invisible at the point of measurement. The delayed resurfacing. What to mandate instead — exposure rather than delegation level.

**Must not claim.** That the model can identify who has been coerced. It cannot, and the piece must not become a pitch for assessment. This is the article most at risk of being read as a product offer for employee screening, and it should include an explicit statement that the framework must not be used that way.

## A6. Using an unvalidated model honestly — for practitioners

**The single idea.** The harm-reversibility test: before applying an untested framework to a person, ask what happens to them if it is wrong.

**Why write this.** It is genuinely useful beyond this model — it applies to every framework circulating in coaching and organisational work, most of which are also untested and few of which say so. It positions the project as unusually careful, which is the reputation most worth having.

**Shape.** The three tiers of claim. The test. The language substitutions that keep use honest. The specific point that expressiveness is not evidence, and that a framework which fits everyone explains nothing.

**Channel.** Coaching and organisational development communities.

## A7. The framework itself — for an academic audience

**The single idea.** A domain-specific position model with modulating conditions, and the studies that would refute it.

This is the preprint from [07-publishing.md](07-publishing.md) rather than an article, but it belongs in the set because it is the version that must exist for anyone to cite the work.

**Shape.** What existing frameworks — technology acceptance, diffusion, transtheoretical models — do and do not cover. What the position framing adds. The falsifiable predictions. A limitations section that is the most detailed part of the paper.

**Must not claim.** Anything empirical. The contribution is the framework and the predictions.

## A8. Why the model dropped its numbering — a short methodological note

**The single idea.** A model whose central claim is that there is no required order cannot use ordinal identifiers, because notation defeats disclaimers.

**Why this is worth writing.** It is a small, self-contained, genuinely transferable point about model design, and it is the kind of piece that gets read and shared by people who build frameworks in other domains. It also demonstrates the project's reasoning quality to readers who will not read the model itself.

**Shape.** The three jobs the identifier was doing. Why renumbering makes it worse — a token that changes meaning between versions corrupts every prior document. What replaced it.

## A9. What changed since the last version — for readers following the work

**The single idea.** A framework describing adaptation to a moving target has to move, and here is what moved.

**Shape.** The five environment shifts: delegation beyond partnership, verification becoming the work, mandated adoption, tool deprecation, and the arrival of an AI-native cohort. What each did to the model.

**Value.** Modest for reach, useful for credibility. It demonstrates that the framework is maintained rather than declared.

# What is deliberately not on this list

| Not written | Reason |
| --- | --- |
| A quiz or "which position are you" piece | The most shareable possible format and the most damaging. It is the mechanism by which an untested framework becomes a label applied to people |
| Anything for a general news audience | The framing would not survive editing. The likely headline is about AI causing psychological harm, which the model does not claim |
| An executive piece on AI-readiness of the workforce | Would be read as a screening proposal regardless of content |
| Advice on moving people between positions | The model is descriptive. See [09-adoption-programme.md](09-adoption-programme.md) for the legitimate version, which targets conditions rather than people |
