## You have a case study and I ignored it

I wrote that no user had been observed. That was wrong — you're a user, and what you describe is precisely the kind of evidence I said would count:

- It changed a **decision** (take a break rather than push through)
- It changed a **self-interpretation** under real distress (a phase rather than a failure)
- It was **forward-looking** — knowing there were next steps and that this one passes

That last part matters most. Description is cheap; the useful thing was the model telling you what came next and that it was temporary. Near-BO is exactly where people push harder and make it worse, because it feels like a character failure. Knowing it was a recognised phase with a known exit is what made the break possible.

The circularity caveat is real — you built the model from your experience, so of course it fits. But **fitting and helping are different claims.** The model fitting your experience is unsurprising. The model helping you act correctly three weeks ago is not, and it's the claim that matters.

That's N=1, unblinded, self-reported. It's also more than I credited, and my audit's method — generating scenarios from the documents — was a weak way to search for value. Absence of evidence from a bad search is close to no information.

## The redundancy question

You're right that "never use it" is not the alternative. The manager decides regardless, and the current inputs — impression, visibility, who speaks up, the last visible mistake — are unvalidated *and* biased. A dice roll isn't the comparison; gut feel is, and gut feel is not obviously better.

But I'd draw a distinction rather than concede the point, because I think it's the useful one:

**Capability is a legitimate criterion. Position is a different variable.**

"Can this person do the work in the way the role now requires?" is a fair question with normal evidence behind it — output, review quality, delivery, adaptability. You don't need the model for it. A person in `GE` Guarded Evaluation about their core domain may be the strongest engineer you have. A person in `IP` Integrated Practice: mastery may be mediocre and enthusiastic. Position doesn't measure the thing the decision needs.

There's also a practical hazard worth knowing: `GE` and `SB` correlate with years invested and depth of craft, which correlates with age. Position as a selection input is a near-textbook route to an age discrimination claim. That's not a moral argument, it's an exposure argument, and it's the one that matters in a redundancy process.

**Where the model genuinely helps that manager** — and this is real, not a consolation prize:

- **It stops premature write-offs.** `GE` and `IS` are phases with known exits. Cutting someone at their worst moment, who would have been fine in a quarter, is expensive and common.
- **It checks whether the conditions were fair.** Was delegation mandated above their ceiling? Was review load dumped on them? If the company manufactured the position, selecting them for it is both unjust and stupid.
- **It tells you what "giving a fair shot" looks like** and what to watch for if it isn't working.
- **`BO` Burnout is recoverable.** Selecting someone in burnout is selecting a good person at their lowest point.

The value there is **procedural fairness, not selection.** It tells you whether you gave the person a real chance before the decision. That protects the company and happens to be the right thing.

## Yes — be openly normative, for a stated purpose

Your horse-rider point is correct and I shouldn't soften it. A software company needing AI-collaborative development has a legitimate preference: `CU` Calibrated Use and `IP` Integrated Practice serve that purpose, `BO` Burnout and prolonged `OU` Overuse: anxiety don't.

The honest formulation isn't "the model is descriptive" — it's:

> **Descriptive about persons. Prescriptive about conditions. Openly normative for a stated purpose, with the purpose named.**

"For a company that needs AI-collaborative development, these are the positions that serve that need. That is a business judgement, not a claim about human flourishing." Nobody is confused by that, and it's more useful than the current oscillation. This is decision D-B1 and I think your version beats the one I recommended.

One exception: `SD` Structural Displacement isn't in the same category as `BO` Burnout. If someone's domain genuinely has no future, recognising that is correct, not a failure state. Some horse riders were right to retrain.

## What transition guidance would actually contain

The core finding, and it's consistent with everything else in the model: **most transitions unlock by changing conditions, not by persuading the person.**

| From → To         | What does not work               | What does                                                                                                                                             |
| ------------------ | -------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `GE` → `CU`   | Argument, evidence, enthusiasm   | Low-stakes domains first. Give them the job of finding the limits — their scepticism is an asset and being right builds trust in their own judgement |
| `IS` → `SB`   | Reassurance, pressure to move on | Naming it. Time. Small wins in adjacent domains                                                                                                       |
| `SB` → `CU`   | Pushing the boundary             | Changing the evidence. The line moves when they test something and it holds                                                                           |
| `OU` → `IP`   | Coaching the individual          | Removing the driver. Mandate or throughput: change the metric. Anxiety: scheduled unaided work to rebuild independent judgement                       |
| `OL` → `IP`   | Time management advice           | Workflow redesign. Sampling, automation, review budgets                                                                                               |
| `BO` → recovery | Re-skilling, encouragement       | Load reduction, rest, and**knowing it's a phase** — your case                                                                                  |

Plus two things a manager needs that the model doesn't currently give: **what reasonable time looks like** for each transition, and **what "not moving" looks like** — the signals that distinguish someone who needs longer from someone for whom this isn't going to work. That second one is what makes the eventual hard decision defensible rather than arbitrary.
